What Is a Discount Calculator?

A discount calculator applies a percentage reduction to a price and shows you all three numbers that matter: what you pay (sale price), what you keep (savings), and confirmation of the discount itself. The core formulas are simple — discount amount = original price × (discount % ÷ 100), and sale price = original price − discount amount — but stores stack complications on top: a second coupon, sales tax, "was/now" pricing that needs reverse-solving.

Good calculators handle the real-world variants. Stacked discounts apply sequentially, not additively — two 20% discounts are not 40% off, they are 36% off ($100 → $80 → $64), because the second discount applies to the already-reduced price. Sales tax applies after the discount in most places, on what you actually pay. And the reverse solver finds the original price from a sale price: original = sale ÷ (1 − discount%/100).

One thing I always check: the "discount %" itself. A $15 saving on a $60 item is 25% off — computing it as (savings ÷ original) × 100 lets you compare a "$20 off" deal against a "30% off" deal honestly, instead of trusting whichever number the sticker shouts louder.

How to Use the Discount Calculator

Enter the original price and the discount percentage. Click Calculate and you get the discount amount and the final sale price instantly. If there is a second discount (a coupon on top of the sale), add it — the tool stacks them in the correct sequential order. Add the sales tax rate if you want the true out-the-door total.

For "was $120, now $90" tags, use the reverse mode: enter both prices and the tool computes the real discount percentage (25% here). I do this routinely with "up to 70% off" sale racks, where the headline discount rarely applies to the item in my hand.

One habit worth building: compare stacked versus single discounts before assuming the bigger headline wins. A 25% store discount plus a 10% coupon beats a flat 30% off — $100 → $75 → $67.50 (32.5% total) versus $70. The calculator shows this in one glance; mental math hides it.

How Discount Calculation Works

Four formulas cover every scenario the tool handles.

Basic discount. Discount = original × (percent ÷ 100); sale price = original − discount. Example: $120 at 25% off → discount $30, sale price $90.

Stacked discounts. Apply one after another: price × (1 − d1) × (1 − d2). Example: $100 with 20% then 20% → $100 × 0.80 × 0.80 = $64. Total saving 36%, not 40%.

With sales tax. Discount first, then tax on the discounted price: total = sale price × (1 + tax%). Example: $90 sale price with 8% tax → $90 × 1.08 = $97.20.

Reverse: find the original. Original = sale ÷ (1 − discount%). Example: sale $40 at 20% off → $40 ÷ 0.80 = $50 original, $10 saved.

Worked example with everything — a $999 TV at 40% off with 8% tax: discount = $999 × 0.40 = $399.60; sale price = $599.40; tax = $599.40 × 0.08 = $47.95; total = $647.35. You saved $399.60 off the sticker — and the tool lays out each line so you can verify it.
