What Is an Open Graph Generator?


Open Graph is a protocol Facebook created in 2010 that lets any page declare how it should appear when shared: its title, description, image, URL, and content type. When someone pastes your link into Facebook, LinkedIn, WhatsApp, Discord, or Slack, the platform reads your page's og: meta tags and builds the preview card from them. Without those tags, each platform guesses — and the guesses are often wrong.


The generator turns that guessing into a form. You enter your og:title (keep it under about 60 characters), og:description (around 150–200 characters works well for social), og:url (the canonical absolute URL), og:image (the preview image — 1200×630 pixels is the recommended size), and og:type (usually "website" for regular pages). It outputs a ready-to-paste block, often with matching Twitter Card tags (twitter:card, twitter:title, and so on) included, since X reads its own tag set. The best generators also render live previews showing your link the way each platform would display it, so you spot a bad image crop before your audience does.


How to Use the Open Graph Generator


The whole job takes a few minutes, and I run it on every important page.


First, pick the page type — Website, Article, Product, or Video — since this sets og:type and can add type-specific properties. For an article, that means article:published_time and author; for a product, price and currency. Then fill in the core fields: title, description, canonical URL, and image URL. Set the image dimensions (default 1200×630) so platforms do not have to probe the image themselves, and add og:site_name plus your locale (e.g. en_US) for completeness.


Check the platform previews. The generator renders mock cards for Facebook, X, LinkedIn, and often Slack or Discord — look at the image crop, read the description at a glance, and confirm the title is not truncated. If anything looks off, adjust and watch the previews update.


Then click copy and paste the tag block into your page's <head>. One detail that bites people: use absolute image URLs (https://…), never relative paths, and keep the image under about 5MB so crawlers do not give up on it.


Finally, validate. Run the URL through Facebook's Sharing Debugger to clear the cached preview and re-scrape, and use X's Card Validator for the Twitter side. Platforms cache aggressively — without the debugger step, your followers may keep seeing the old broken preview for days.

Two common mistakes I see constantly: missing og:image (the card renders as plain text, which kills click-through), and relative image URLs that the platform cannot resolve. The generator defaults guard against both, but double-check after pasting.

<meta name="twitter:card" content="summary_large_image">

Frequently asked




What is Open Graph? 
Open Graph is a protocol (created by Facebook in 2010) that lets a web page declare how it should appear when shared — its title, description, image, and URL. Social platforms read these og: meta tags to build link preview cards.


What size should my og:image be? 
1200×630 pixels is the recommended size. Use an absolute HTTPS URL and keep the file under about 5MB so platform crawlers fetch it reliably.


Why does my shared link show the wrong preview? 
Social platforms cache previews aggressively. After fixing your tags, run the URL through Facebook's Sharing Debugger (and X's Card Validator) to clear the cache and force a re-scrape.


Do I need Twitter Card tags if I have Open Graph? 
Mostly yes for X. X can fall back to Open Graph tags, but explicit twitter:card tags — especially summary_large_image — give you reliable control over the big image card format.


Does og:description need to differ from the meta description? 
Not necessarily. The generator defaults it to your SEO description, which is fine. Use a different one only if the social share should read differently — punchier, more conversational — than the search listing.


Is this generator free? 
Yes — completely free, no account needed.


Where do I paste the generated tags? 
Inside the <head> section of your HTML. In WordPress, SEO plugins can manage Open Graph tags for you, but the generator gives you exact control for static sites and custom builds.


Can I use a relative URL for og:image? 
No — always use the absolute URL starting with https://. Platform crawlers cannot resolve relative paths, and the image will silently fail to load.
