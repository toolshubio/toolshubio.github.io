An SEO URL checker analyzes a URL against the practices Google actually rewards: short, readable, lowercase, hyphenated, keyword-bearing. Paste a URL, get a verdict on each rule, and fix what fails.

What Is an SEO URL Checker?

Your URL is the first thing a searcher reads about your page, and one of the first things Google's crawler parses. A clean URL — example.com/best-budget-laptops — signals relevance at a glance; a messy one — example.com/p?id=4829&session=xyz — signals nothing and earns fewer clicks. The checker is a tool that scores a URL against the established best practices and reports pass or fail on each: length, lowercase usage, hyphens instead of underscores, keyword presence, absence of stop words and parameters, HTTPS, and readable structure.

URLs are a minor ranking factor, but a real one — and more importantly, they are a click-through factor. When your URL appears in search results, in a shared link, or in an email, a readable slug builds trust before the page even loads. The checker turns vague advice ("make your URLs SEO-friendly") into a concrete list of green checks and red flags.

How to Use the SEO URL Checker

The check takes seconds, and I run it on every new page before publishing.

Enter the full URL into the checker's input box and hit Analyze. The tool returns an overall status plus a breakdown of individual checks: Is it under the recommended length? All lowercase? Words separated by hyphens? Does it contain the target keyword? Free of unnecessary parameters, session IDs, and stop words? Using HTTPS?

Fix the red flags one by one. The most common repairs: rewriting the slug to include the primary keyword (/gwa-calculator-philippines beats /page-42), replacing underscores with hyphens (Google treats hyphens as word separators; underscores join words into one token), cutting the slug to the essential 3–5 words, and stripping tracking parameters from the canonical version. If the page already ranks and gets traffic, do not change the URL casually — set up a 301 redirect from the old URL to the new one so you keep the link equity.

Re-run the check after editing. When every box is green, you have a URL that reads well to humans and parses cleanly to crawlers.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do URLs affect SEO rankings?

They are a minor ranking factor — a keyword in the slug helps a little. The bigger win is click-through rate: readable URLs get clicked more in results and shared links, and engagement compounds.

Should I use hyphens or underscores?

Hyphens. Google treats hyphens as word separators (budget-laptops = "budget laptops") but reads underscores as joiners (budget_laptops = one token). This is one of the few URL rules Google has stated explicitly.

How long should a URL be?

Keep the slug to 3–5 meaningful words. Shorter URLs are easier to read, share, and remember — and they display in full in search results instead of getting cut off.

Should I change the URL of a page that already ranks?

Usually not. If you must, use a 301 redirect from the old URL to the new one and update internal links. Changing a ranking URL without a redirect throws away its history.

Do stop words hurt in URLs?

They do not hurt rankings, but they add length without meaning. Dropping "the," "and," "of," and "a" keeps slugs tight — /sharpen-kitchen-knife reads better than /how-to-sharpen-a-kitchen-knife.

Are URL parameters bad for SEO?

For the canonical URL, yes — parameters create duplicate-content risk and look untrustworthy. Use clean slugs for the canonical version and keep parameters (tracking, sessions) out of it.

Does my URL data leave my browser?

No. The analysis runs entirely client-side; nothing is uploaded or stored.
