Color Palette — Extract a Color Palette From Any Image, Free

You see a photo with a color scheme you love — the warm dusk tones in a travel shot, the exact blue in a competitor's logo, the muted greens of a product photo — and you want those colors in your own project. Eyeballing hex codes never quite lands. A color palette extractor analyzes the image and pulls out its dominant colors as ready-to-use swatches with hex, RGB, and HSL values. Upload the image, get the palette, copy what you need — free, in seconds, in your browser.

What Is a Color Palette Extractor?

It is a tool that turns any picture into a usable set of colors. Under the hood, the technique is called color quantization: the tool samples pixels from a downscaled copy of your image, groups similar colors into clusters, and surfaces the dominant ones ranked by visual weight. Each swatch earns its place — no filler shades.

Designers call it many names: image color picker, dominant color extractor, photo color scheme generator, color grabber. Whatever the name, the output is the same — a balanced palette you can drop into a design system, brand guide, CSS file, or mood board instead of guessing.

When I first used one, what struck me was how it removed arguments. Two people can debate "which blue is in that photo" forever; the extractor just shows you the data. For teams, that is worth more than the time saved.

How to Extract Colors From an Image

Step 1 — Upload your image. Drag and drop, click to browse, or paste straight from your clipboard. JPG, PNG, GIF, WebP, and BMP all work.

Step 2 — Set the color count. Choose how many swatches you want — anywhere from 2 to 12 or even 30 depending on the tool. Start with 6–8 for a balanced palette; go lower for focused UI themes, higher for illustration work. A diversity setting can force more visually distinct shades instead of near-identical neighbors.

Step 3 — Review and copy. Click any swatch to copy its hex code instantly; most tools also show RGB, HSL, and sometimes OKLCH or Lab values. Lock favorites and re-extract if you want a different mix.

Step 4 — Export. Download the palette in the format your workflow expects: CSS variables, Tailwind config, JSON tokens, SVG strip, PNG swatches, or professional formats like Adobe .aco/.ase, Procreate .swatches, and GIMP .gpl.

Habit worth building: ignore-white and transparent-background options. Product photos on white backgrounds will otherwise hand you five shades of off-white as your "palette."

Developers convert extracted colors into design tokens — CSS custom properties, Tailwind configs, JSON — and wire them straight into a codebase.

Marketers build campaign palettes from product photography so ads, landing pages, and social posts share one coherent color story.

Practical Tips for Better Palettes

  • Pick images with clear color stories. A sunset photo yields a gorgeous palette; a cluttered screenshot yields mud.
  • Exclude backgrounds. Use the ignore-white/transparent option so backdrops do not dominate the swatches.
  • Nudge toward brand colors. Extracted colors are editable — shift a swatch slightly toward your existing brand before exporting.
  • Test at real sizes. A color that sings as a swatch can overwhelm as a full background — mock it in context.
  • Check contrast. Run swatches through a WCAG checker before using them for text or UI elements.
  • Export, do not screenshot. Copy the actual hex values or export tokens — eyeballing from a screenshot reintroduces the guessing you just eliminated.
  • Sample multiple images. One photo gives a palette; three photos from the same shoot give a system.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the color palette extractor free?

Yes — completely free, no account required.

How are the palette colors chosen?

The tool samples pixels from a downscaled copy of your image, groups similar colors (color quantization), and ranks the most prominent, visually distinct clusters for a clean, usable palette.

What formats can I export to?

CSS variables, Tailwind config, JSON tokens, CSV, SVG strips, and PNG swatches — plus professional formats like Adobe .aco/.ase, Procreate .swatches, GIMP .gpl, and Clip Studio Paint .cls.

Are my images uploaded to a server?

No. All color analysis happens locally in your browser — nothing is uploaded, stored, or seen by anyone else.

How many colors should I extract?

Start with 6–8 for a balanced palette. Use fewer (3–4) for focused UI themes, more (10–12+) for illustration or mood-board work. You can re-extract with different counts freely.

Can I copy hex and RGB values?

Yes — click any swatch to copy its hex code instantly, with RGB, HSL, and sometimes OKLCH/Lab values shown alongside.

My palette is all whites and grays — what went wrong?

The tool sampled your background. Enable the ignore-white or ignore-transparent option and re-extract — the real colors will surface.

Can I use extracted colors commercially?

Colors themselves are not copyrightable — extracting a palette from an image is fine. Just make sure you have rights to the source image if you are reproducing it beyond its colors.
