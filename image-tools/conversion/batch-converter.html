I once inherited a folder with 400 product photos — a mix of PNGs, BMPs, and TIFFs — that all needed to become web-ready JPGs before lunch. Converting them one at a time would have eaten my entire morning. Instead I dragged the whole folder into a batch converter, picked JPG, and had a ZIP of finished files in minutes. That is the entire point of batch conversion: the same settings, applied to every file, in a single pass.

What Is a Batch Converter?

A batch converter processes multiple image files at once instead of one by one. You upload dozens or hundreds of images, choose a target format (JPG, PNG, WebP, and others), optionally set quality or resize options, and the tool converts every file with identical settings. The results download individually or as a single ZIP archive.

The difference from a single-file converter is purely operational — one settings screen, one click, one download — but the time savings scale brutally. Five images? Barely matters. Fifty? You just saved an hour of repetitive clicking. Five hundred? You just saved your week.

How to Use the Batch Converter

Step 1 — Select all your images. Drag and drop the whole set onto the page, or click to multi-select. Most tools accept mixed input formats in a single batch — JPGs, PNGs, BMPs, TIFFs, WebP, HEIC — and convert them all to one target format.

Step 2 — Choose format and options. Pick your output format, set the quality slider (80–90 for a good balance), and optionally resize — by percentage or to a maximum width. Resize-at-convert is the quiet killer feature: it turns 24-megapixel originals into lean web images in the same pass.

Step 3 — Convert and download. One click starts the whole batch. Progress is shown per file, and when everything finishes you download each image separately or grab the full set as a ZIP.

Supported Formats

A good batch converter reads broadly and writes to the formats that matter. Typical input: JPG, PNG, WebP, GIF, BMP, TIFF, ICO, SVG, HEIC, and AVIF. Typical output: JPG, PNG, and WebP cover nearly every real-world need — JPG for photos and compatibility, PNG for graphics and transparency, WebP for the smallest web-ready files.

Note the conversion direction matters for quality. Converting between lossless formats (BMP → PNG, TIFF → PNG) loses nothing. Converting to a lossy format (anything → JPG) discards some data permanently. Keep your originals archived before running a batch — a bulk operation applied to your only copies is a bulk mistake.

Tips for Large Batches

Test on three files first. Convert a small sample, zoom in, and check the quality before unleashing the settings on 500 images. A wrong quality choice is cheap to fix on three files and painful on three hundred.

Mind your browser's memory. Browser-based converters process locally, which is private and fast — but a hundred 20 MB photos can strain a low-RAM device. Split giant batches into chunks of 30–50 if things slow down.

Name files sensibly before converting. "IMG_20261011_094231.jpg" tells you nothing six months later. Rename the batch with a project prefix before you convert, so the ZIP you download is already organized.

Strip metadata for public files. Photos carry EXIF data — camera model, date, sometimes GPS location. Stripping it before publishing is basic hygiene; keeping it is right for archives. Decide per batch.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the batch converter free?

Yes — free, with no account or signup required.

How many images can I convert at once?

Dozens to hundreds, depending on file sizes and your device's memory. Split very large batches into smaller groups if needed.

Can I mix formats in one batch?

Yes. Upload PNGs, BMPs, TIFFs, and WebPs together — they all convert to your chosen output format.

Will quality be reduced?

Only when converting to a lossy format like JPG. Converting between lossless formats (BMP → PNG) loses nothing. Quality slider controls the tradeoff.

Are my images uploaded to a server?

No — conversion runs locally in your browser, so your files never leave your device.

How do I download the results?

Download files individually or all at once as a single ZIP archive.

Can I resize images during conversion?

Yes — set a maximum width, height, or percentage scale and it applies to the whole batch.

Should I batch-convert my originals?

No. Always work on copies and keep your originals archived — especially before lossy conversion.

Frequently asked

What does the batch converter do differently?
Instead of converting one file to one format, it converts many files to many formats at once — e.g. 20 photos into JPG, WebP and PNG versions in a single run.
Can inputs be different formats?
Yes. Mixed batches (PNG, JPG, WebP, AVIF...) are supported; each file is converted to your chosen output formats.
Is there a limit on batch size?
The practical limit is your device's memory and CPU. Dozens of typical photos process smoothly.
Do my files get uploaded?
No — everything is processed locally in your browser. Nothing leaves your device.
