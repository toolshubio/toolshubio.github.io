What Is a Small Text Generator?

A small text generator replaces each letter you type with a smaller-looking Unicode character, producing tiny text in styles like small caps, superscript, and subscript. "Hello" can become "ʜᴇʟʟᴏ", "ʰᵉˡˡᵒ", or "ₕₑₗₗₒ" depending on the style you pick. The output is not a shrunken font — it is a different set of real characters — so the tiny look survives copy-paste into Instagram bios, TikTok captions, WhatsApp messages, and anywhere else plain text goes.

How to Use the Small Text Generator

Type or paste your text into the input box and the tool shows all the small styles at once — small caps, superscript, subscript, sometimes more. Click the copy button next to the style you like, then paste it into your bio, post, comment, or chat.

Keep it short. Tiny text is a seasoning, not a main course: a username, a tagline, or a one-line joke lands perfectly, while a whole paragraph in miniature becomes hard to read and annoying to your audience.

Superscript and subscript will look familiar from math and science — the 2 in m² and the 2 in H₂O are the same idea. The generator just applies that raised-and-lowered look to whole words.

How Unicode Makes It Possible

Computers do not store letters as shapes; they store numbers, and Unicode assigns a unique number to every character. Tucked inside the standard are dedicated ranges — Superscripts and Subscripts (U+2070–U+209F) and Phonetic Extensions (U+1D00–U+1D7F) — full of miniature letter variants. The generator simply maps each of your letters to its tiny counterpart.

Because these are standard characters, they work on most modern phones, browsers, and apps with no font installation. The catch: Unicode does not include a tiny version of every letter. A few characters — like small-cap X, or several subscript letters — have no equivalent, so the generator leaves them unchanged. If your output has one stubborn normal-size letter in the middle, that is why.

Frequently asked

What is the difference between superscript and subscript?
Superscript characters sit above the baseline (like exponents), subscript characters sit below (like chemical formulas).
Where is small text used?
Social bios, decorative headings, math-style notation and playful messages.
Are all letters supported?
Most letters are; a few characters have no tiny Unicode form and stay normal.
Is it free?
