Image Splitter — Split Photos Into Equal Parts Online Free

That seamless 9-tile grid on an Instagram profile — one big image spread across nine posts — is not nine separate designs. It is one image, sliced into nine squares. An image splitter does the slicing: upload a photo, choose rows and columns, and download the pieces individually or as a ZIP. This free tool runs the whole job in your browser, supports grids up to 50 × 50, and keeps every piece at full original resolution.

What Is an Image Splitter?

An image splitter divides one picture into multiple equal parts. The classic setup is a grid — 3 × 3 for the nine-tile Instagram feed, 2 × 2 for four quadrants — but it also handles plain horizontal strips (top/bottom halves) and vertical columns (left/right slices), and some tools even offer angled cuts with parallel slanted lines.

The use cases are more varied than I expected. Social media grids are the headline, but print shops slice posters into printable sections, teachers split maps and diagrams for classroom walls, game developers cut sprite sheets into predictable tiles, and photographers divide panoramas for multi-frame wall displays. Anywhere one image needs to become many equal files, this is the tool.

Each piece downloads as its own image — named in reading order, so photo-part-1 is the top-left tile — and everything keeps the full resolution of its area. Nothing is re-compressed into softness; a tile is exactly as sharp as the region it came from.

How to Split an Image Online

Step 1 — Upload your image. Drop the file or click to select. JPG, PNG, WebP, GIF, and BMP all work.

Step 2 — Pick rows and columns. The default 3 × 3 gives nine equal squares — the standard Instagram feed grid. Set any configuration from 1 × 1 up to 50 × 50 (yes, a full 2500-cell tile mosaic is possible).

Step 3 — Adjust the crop area. Move and zoom the cropper to choose which part of the image gets split. The aspect-ratio lock keeps the cropper proportional so the cells come out even — important when the source is not the same shape as your grid.

Step 4 — Preview and download. The preview shows numbered thumbnails of every piece. Download each part on its own or grab the whole set as one ZIP file.

For uneven needs, drag individual split lines — most tools let you move a line anywhere between 5% and 95% of the image, so you can cut a big hero piece with smaller tiles beside it rather than a perfectly even grid.

Practical Tips for Clean Splits

  • Start from high resolution. Nine tiles from a 1080px image are 360px each — fine for a feed grid, weak for print. Bigger source, better tiles.
  • Mind the gaps. Instagram shows tiny gutters between grid tiles; keep important details away from tile edges so nothing critical gets visually cut.
  • Number your exports. Reading-order naming (part-1 = top-left) keeps posting order obvious — download the ZIP and post in sequence.
  • Choose format deliberately. PNG for graphics and text-heavy designs (lossless edges), JPG for photos (smaller files), WebP for web use.
  • Sharpen before splitting. A light sharpness pass on the full image carries through to every tile uniformly.
  • Resize tiles after, not before. Split at full resolution, then bulk resize the pieces if a platform wants specific tile dimensions.
  • Test-print one row. For physical mosaics, print a single row at low quality first to verify alignment before the full high-quality run.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the image splitter free?

Yes — 100% free, no signup, no watermarks on the downloaded pieces, no limit on how many images you split.

How do I split an image into 9 parts for Instagram?

Upload your photo, keep the default 3 × 3 grid, download the nine pieces as a ZIP, then post them in reverse order (piece #9 first, piece #1 last) so they display correctly on your profile.

Are my images uploaded to a server?

No. Splitting runs entirely in your browser via JavaScript — your photos never leave your device, so nothing about them exists anywhere but on your own machine.

Can I split an image into unequal parts?

Yes. Drag individual split lines to reposition them (typically anywhere from 5% to 95% of the image), so you can cut, for example, one large hero piece with smaller tiles beside it.

What formats can I upload and download?

Upload JPG, PNG, WebP, GIF, or BMP. Download pieces as PNG (lossless, best for graphics), JPG (smallest for photos), or WebP (best balance for web).

Do the pieces keep full quality?

Yes — every piece is saved at the full original resolution of its area. A tile is exactly as sharp as the region it came from.

Can I split an image vertically or horizontally only?

Yes. Horizontal mode divides the image into stacked sections (top/bottom), vertical mode into columns (left/right), and grid mode into rows × columns. Angled parallel cuts are available in some tools.

Can I merge the parts back together later?

Yes — an image merger rejoins tiles into one file. Just keep the pieces at matching sizes and the seam will be invisible.
