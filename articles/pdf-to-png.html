Why PNG Instead of JPG?

PNG compression is lossless — every pixel is preserved exactly, no matter how many times you open, edit, and resave the file. JPG, by contrast, re-compresses lossily each save, and its artifacts show up first on exactly the content PDFs are full of: sharp text edges, thin lines, flat color areas, and high-contrast diagrams.

PNG also supports transparency, which JPG cannot do. If your PDF pages have transparent elements — logos on transparent backgrounds, layered graphics — PNG keeps that alpha channel intact, so the image composites cleanly over any background on a web page.

The price is file size. A PNG of a detailed page can be two to five times larger than the equivalent JPG. For diagrams, screenshots, and text pages the difference is modest and worth it; for full-page photographs, JPG's smaller size usually wins. My rule: text and line art go PNG, photos go JPG.

How to Use the PDF to PNG Converter

Drop your PDF into the tool and choose your pages — all, a range, or a single page. Set the output resolution: 150 DPI for screen and web use, 300 DPI for print or detailed inspection. PNG has no quality slider (there is nothing to degrade), so DPI is your main control.

Hit Convert and download the images, individually or as a ZIP. My check: zoom to 100% and look at text edges and thin lines — they should be razor sharp with no halos or smudging. If the image looks soft, the DPI was too low, not the format; reconvert higher.

One tip for web use: PNGs at 300 DPI are overkill for screens and slow down page loads. Convert at 150 DPI (or even 96–120 for thumbnails) when the destination is a website — you keep the lossless sharpness at a sensible file size.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my PNG bigger than a JPG of the same page?

PNG is lossless — it keeps every pixel exactly, which takes more bytes. For text and diagrams the sharpness is worth it; for photos, JPG is smaller.

Does PNG keep transparent backgrounds?

Yes. PNG preserves the alpha channel, so transparent elements from the PDF stay transparent in the image.

Can I convert just one page?

Yes — select a single page or a range rather than the whole document.

What DPI should I use for a website?

96–150 DPI. Higher DPI only makes the file bigger without visible benefit on screens.

Will repeated edits degrade the PNG?

No — that is the point of lossless. Open, edit, and resave as many times as you like with zero quality loss.
