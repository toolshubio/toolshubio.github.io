What Is the AVIF Format?

AVIF (AV1 Image File Format) is a modern image format built on the AV1 video codec, developed by the Alliance for Open Media — a group that includes Google, Netflix, Apple, and Meta. It was designed to be royalty-free and brutally efficient: AVIF files run roughly 50% smaller than JPEG at the same visual quality, and about 20% smaller than WebP. It also supports features JPEG never had — transparency, HDR, wide color gamuts, 10- and 12-bit color depth, and even animation.

The catch is compatibility. Browsers added support gradually — Chrome 85 in 2020, Firefox 93 in 2021, Safari 16 in 2022 — and the long tail is enormous. Older software, email clients, design tools predating 2022, content management systems, and print services mostly still do not read AVIF. Where did you probably get one? CDNs like Cloudflare and social platforms serve AVIF automatically to browsers that support it, so right-click-saving an image in Chrome often lands you an .avif file without warning.

Why Convert AVIF to JPG?

  • Universal compatibility — JPG opens in every browser, app, phone, and printer made in the last thirty years.
  • Editing — older Photoshop, GIMP builds, and most desktop editors do not handle AVIF; JPG works everywhere.
  • Uploads and forms — CMS platforms, job portals, and print services accept JPG and reject AVIF.
  • Sharing — email clients and messaging apps that choke on AVIF display JPG without complaint.
  • Printing — virtually no print workflow accepts AVIF; JPG is the expected input.

The conversion is one-way in spirit: AVIF wins on file size, JPG wins on ubiquity. Convert for compatibility, and keep the AVIF original archived if it matters.

Step 2 — Set the JPG quality. A quality slider (usually 1–100) controls the tradeoff. 90 is the default and looks practically identical to the source for photos; 75 is fine for thumbnails. Tips for Best Results

Keep the AVIF original. JPG is lossy, and every re-save costs a little detail. The AVIF is your master; the JPG is the delivery copy.

Watch the transparency fill. If the AVIF had a transparent background, white is the default fill — fine for documents, wrong for dark-themed designs. Choose a fill that matches the destination.

Batch when you have many. Saved-image folders accumulate AVIFs fast. Converting the whole folder at once and downloading a ZIP beats doing it one by one.

Consider PNG for graphics. If the AVIF is a logo or illustration with sharp edges and transparency, PNG (lossless, transparency-preserving) may serve you better than JPG.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will converting lose quality?

At JPG quality 90 or higher, the difference is invisible in normal viewing. AVIF's HDR and 10-bit color advantages are lost, so archive the original for critical work.

What happens to transparency?

JPG cannot store transparency, so transparent areas are filled with a background color (white by default). Choose the fill that suits your use.

Can I convert multiple AVIF files at once?

Yes — batch conversion is supported, with results downloadable as a ZIP.

Why do my saved images keep coming as AVIF?

Websites and CDNs serve AVIF to browsers that support it. When you right-click and save, you get the format the site sent. Convert to JPG for universal use.

Are my files uploaded to a server?

Browser-based converters process everything locally — your images never leave your device.

What is the difference between AVIF and HEIC?

Both are modern, efficient formats. HEIC uses HEVC (H.265) compression and is Apple's iPhone default; AVIF uses AV1 compression and is favored by browsers and CDNs. Both convert to JPG the same way.

Can I convert JPG back to AVIF?

Yes, but it gains nothing — the detail already discarded by JPEG compression cannot be recovered.
