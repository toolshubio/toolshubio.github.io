What Is an Electronic Signature?

An electronic signature (e-signature) is any digital mark that indicates your intent to sign — a drawn signature, a typed name in a script font, or an uploaded image of your handwriting. It's the digital equivalent of signing with a pen, and for contracts, NDAs, offer letters, and agreements, it's legally binding in the US, EU, and most of the world.

One distinction worth knowing: "electronic signature" and "digital signature" aren't quite the same thing. An electronic signature is the broad category — any intent-to-sign mark. A digital signature is a specific type that uses cryptographic certificates to verify identity and detect tampering. All digital signatures are electronic signatures, but not vice versa. For everyday agreements, a standard e-signature is what you need; regulated industries sometimes require the cryptographic kind.

A few document types are typically excluded from e-signature laws — wills, adoption papers, and court orders among them. When in doubt about a high-stakes document, check your jurisdiction's rules. For the other 99% of paperwork, signing online is fine.

How to Use the Document Signer

Upload your document — PDF, image, or other supported format — by dragging it onto the upload area or clicking to browse. It renders in the signing canvas, ready for your mark.

Create your signature on the drawing pad. Use your mouse, touchpad, finger, or stylus — a stylus or finger on a touchscreen gives the most natural result, but a careful mouse signature works fine. Adjust the pen color (classic blue or black) and pen width until the stroke looks like you. Messed up a stroke? Hit clear and redraw; it takes most people two or three tries to get one they like.

Place the signature where it belongs — the signature line, naturally, but also initials boxes and date fields if your document has them. Resize and position it precisely, then download the signed file. The signature becomes part of the document, ready to email back.

My tip: practice the signature a few times before placing it on the real document. And sign with the same general style each time — consistency matters if anyone ever compares signatures across your documents.
