What Makes SVG Different From JPG and PNG

JPG and PNG are raster formats: grids of pixels. Enlarge them and they blur. SVG is vector: mathematical descriptions of shapes, lines, and text. Enlarge an SVG ten times and every edge stays razor sharp, because the image is redrawn from its geometry rather than stretched. For logos, icons, diagrams, and typography, this is transformative — one file serves a favicon and a billboard.

SVGs are also tiny for the content they suit. A logo that is 2 MB as a PNG might be 20 KB as an SVG. And they are editable: open one in Illustrator, Figma, or even a text editor, and you can recolor shapes, adjust curves, and change text. Try that with a JPG.

The honest caveat: SVG conversion only produces true vectors from vector content. If your PDF page is a scan — a photograph of a page — there are no vectors inside to extract, and the "SVG" will just be the raster image wrapped in an SVG container. It will work, but it will not scale magically. Vector PDFs (exported from Illustrator, InDesign, CAD, or Office apps) are where this tool shines.

How to Use the PDF to SVG Converter

Drop your PDF into the tool and select the pages you want — SVG conversion is usually a page-at-a-time or selected-pages affair, since each page becomes its own SVG file. Hit Convert and download the results.

My check: open the SVG in a browser and zoom in hard — 400%, 800%. True vector content stays perfectly sharp at any zoom; if edges blur, the source page was raster and you have an embedded image, not vectors. Also open it in a vector editor if you plan to edit: text may come through as editable text or as outlined paths depending on the tool, and knowing which saves surprises later.

For web use, run the SVG through an optimizer afterward if file size matters — converters sometimes emit verbose SVGs, and a quick optimization pass can halve the size with no visual change.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will my SVG scale without losing quality?

Yes, if the PDF contained vector content — shapes and text redraw perfectly at any size. Scanned pages become embedded raster images and will not scale.

Can I edit the SVG afterward?

Yes. Open it in any vector editor (Figma, Illustrator, Inkscape) to recolor, reshape, and retype. Text may arrive as editable text or outlined paths.

Why is my SVG blurry when zoomed?

The source page was raster (a scan or photo), so the SVG just wraps that image. True vectors never blur — check your PDF's origin.

Is SVG good for photographs?

No. SVG excels at logos, icons, diagrams, and text. Photographs should stay JPG or PNG.

Can I convert only one page?

Yes — select individual pages or a range; each becomes its own SVG file.
