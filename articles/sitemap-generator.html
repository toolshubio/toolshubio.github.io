What Is a Sitemap Generator?

An XML sitemap is a file (usually at yourdomain.com/sitemap.xml) that lists every URL on your site you want search engines to crawl, with metadata about each: when it was last updated (lastmod), how often it changes (changefreq), and its relative priority. Instead of relying purely on internal links to find your pages, Google gets a clean list — a discovery shortcut.

The generator creates this file automatically. You give it your domain (or upload a list of URLs), it crawls your site up to a page limit — free tools typically cover 100 to 500 pages per run — and produces valid XML you can download, upload to your site root, and submit in Search Console. Sitemaps become essential for sites with deep page structures, sites that publish frequently, e-commerce stores with thousands of product pages, multilingual sites with hreflang, and any site recovering from indexing problems. One honest note: sitemap inclusion supports discovery, but it does not guarantee a page will be crawled or indexed.

How to Use the Sitemap Generator

The process is short, and most of it is waiting for the crawl.

Enter your website's URL into the generator and choose your crawl settings — depth and max pages. Some tools also accept a CSV with one URL per row, which is faster for large sites where you already know the URLs. Click generate and let it crawl; for a small site this takes under a minute.

Review the results before downloading. A good generator reports which URLs were included and which were excluded, with warnings. Exclude anything that should not be there: duplicate alternatives, redirecting URLs, error pages, and anything marked noindex. The sitemap should contain only preferred, canonical URLs — redirecting and non-canonical URLs should be left out so the file points to final pages only.

Download the XML file, upload it to your site's root directory (so it answers at yourdomain.com/sitemap.xml), and submit it in Google Search Console under Sitemaps. Remove old or invalid sitemaps while you are there. Then regenerate and resubmit whenever you add significant new content — or better, let a plugin handle it automatically.

What Goes in the File

ElementWhat it meansExample
<loc>The page's full canonical URLhttps://example.com/about
<lastmod>Date the page last changed2026-10-01
<changefreq>How often it typically changesweekly, monthly, yearly
<priority>Relative importance, 0.0–1.01.00 for homepage, 0.80 for key pages

A minimal valid entry looks like this:

<url>
  <loc>https://www.example.com/about</loc>
  <lastmod>2026-10-05</lastmod>
  <changefreq>monthly</changefreq>
  <priority>0.80</priority>
</url>

The generator writes all of this for you — no XML knowledge needed. Keep the file under 50,000 URLs and 50MB uncompressed; larger sites split into multiple sitemaps with a sitemap index file.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an XML sitemap?

A file at your site's root listing every URL you want search engines to crawl, with metadata like last-modified dates and change frequency. It helps Google discover and crawl your pages efficiently.

Does a sitemap guarantee indexing?

No. It helps discovery, but Google still decides what to crawl and index. Think of it as handing over a map — the crawler still chooses the route.

Which URLs should I include?

Only preferred, canonical URLs you want indexed. Exclude duplicates, redirects, error pages, and noindex pages — a clean sitemap is a useful sitemap.

Where do I put the sitemap file?

In your site's root directory, so it answers at yourdomain.com/sitemap.xml. Then submit that URL in Google Search Console under Sitemaps.

How often should I update my sitemap?

Regenerate whenever you add or remove significant pages. WordPress SEO plugins do this automatically; static sites need a manual regenerate or a build-step generator.

What is the URL limit for a sitemap?

50,000 URLs and 50MB uncompressed per file. Larger sites use multiple sitemaps listed in a sitemap index file.

Do I need a sitemap if my site is small?

It is still good practice — it costs nothing and speeds up discovery. Small sites benefit most when pages are new and have few inbound links.

Is this generator free?

Yes — most online generators are free for small sites (typically up to 100–500 pages per run) with no signup required.
