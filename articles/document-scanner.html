What Is a Document Scanner?

A document scanner is software that converts photos of physical documents into proper digital scans. Point your camera at a page and the tool does four things a raw photo can't: it finds the document's corners automatically, corrects the perspective so a photo taken at an angle becomes a flat rectangle, enhances contrast to make text crisp, and removes shadows and glare.

The result looks like it came from a flatbed scanner — black text on a clean white background — instead of a crooked phone photo with your thumb in the corner. Multi-page mode lets you scan a stack of pages into a single PDF, which is how contracts, receipts, and notebooks become emailable documents.

OCR is the second half of the magic. It reads the text in your scan and converts it into actual selectable characters. A scanned contract becomes searchable ("find the termination clause"), copyable (paste a paragraph into an email), and editable. Without OCR, a scan is just a picture of text; with it, it's a document.

How to Use the Document Scanner

Click the camera button to capture a page directly, or upload existing photos from your device. The scanner detects the page corners automatically — if it misses (curled pages and busy backgrounds confuse it), adjust the corner handles manually until the outline hugs the page edges.

Fine-tune the image with the brightness and contrast sliders. A photo taken in warm indoor light often needs a contrast boost to make text pop; a washed-out page needs brightness pulled down. The goal is dark text on a clean background — preview the result before moving on.

Scanning multiple pages? Hit "add more" and capture each page; they collect in the pages strip where you can reorder or remove any of them. When the set is complete, choose your export format (PDF for documents, image when you need a picture) and download. For searchable text, run OCR on the scan — the extracted text appears in the results panel with a word count, ready to copy.

My setup for reliable scans: lay the page flat on a dark, uncluttered surface (contrast helps edge detection), shoot in bright indirect light to avoid shadows, and hold the phone parallel to the page. Two extra seconds of care here saves minutes of cleanup later.

The blur point deserves emphasis: OCR engines are remarkably tolerant of skew, shadows, and mediocre lighting, but motion blur destroys character shapes beyond recognition. If your OCR output is gibberish, retake the photo steadier before blaming the software.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this document scanner free?

Yes — free, no signup, no watermarks on your scans.

Do I need to install anything?

No. It runs in your browser on any device with a camera — phone, tablet, or laptop.

How does the scanner straighten my photo?

It detects the page's four corners, then applies a perspective transform that maps the angled photo onto a flat rectangle — the same math flatbed scanners avoid needing.

What is OCR and how accurate is it?

OCR (optical character recognition) converts the text in your scan into selectable characters. On clean printed text it's typically 95%+ accurate; handwriting and blur lower that.

Why is my OCR output gibberish?

Almost always a blurry or shadowed photo. Retake it steadier with better light, boost contrast, and try again.

Can I scan multiple pages into one PDF?

Yes — use "add more" to capture each page, reorder them in the pages strip, and export as a single PDF.

What should I scan as PDF vs. image?

PDF for documents you'll share, file, or print; image format when you need a picture for a presentation or message.

Are my scans uploaded anywhere?

No. Capture, enhancement, and OCR all happen in your browser; your documents stay on your device.
