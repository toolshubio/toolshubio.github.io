What Is a Hex to Decimal Converter?

Hexadecimal is base-16, using digits 0–9 plus A–F (where A=10 through F=15). Decimal is our everyday base-10. Converting means evaluating the hex digits as powers of 16: in the value 1A3, the 1 is worth 1×256, the A is worth 10×16, and the 3 is worth 3×1 — totaling 419. The converter does that positional math for you.

You encounter hex values needing translation in debuggers (memory addresses), color tools (hex color codes), error codes, MAC addresses, and file headers. Each of these is more compact than decimal, but when you need to compare magnitudes or do arithmetic, decimal is the language your brain thinks in.

One thing that catches people: hex 10 is not ten, it is sixteen. And hex 100 is not one hundred, it is 256. The place values grow by sixteens (1, 16, 256, 4096), which is why hex numbers "look small" while being large.

How Hex to Decimal Conversion Works

One formula, applied left to right or right to left — your choice.

The positional formula. For hex digits dₙ₋₁…d₁d₀: decimal = dₙ₋₁×16ⁿ⁻¹ + … + d₁×16¹ + d₀×16⁰. Convert each letter to its value first (A=10 … F=15).

Worked example — 1A3. Positions from the right: 16⁰=1, 16¹=16, 16²=256. Compute: 1×256 + 10×16 + 3×1 = 256 + 160 + 3 = 419.

Worked example — E7A9. Four digits: 14×4096 + 7×256 + 10×16 + 9×1 = 57344 + 1792 + 160 + 9 = 59305. Long by hand, instant in the tool.

A shortcut for small values: memorize that the rightmost digit is ones, the next is sixteens, then two-hundred-fifty-sixes. Two-digit hex becomes a quick mental multiplication — 3B is 3×16+11 = 59 — which covers a lot of real-world reading.

How do I convert hex to decimal manually?

Multiply each digit by its power of 16 (1, 16, 256, 4096 from the right) and add. For 1A3: 1×256 + 10×16 + 3 = 419.

What is FF in decimal?

255 — the maximum value of one byte.
