What Is a Meta Robots Generator?

Search engines send crawlers to read your pages, and the meta robots tag is how you give each individual page instructions about what to do with it. The tag sits in the <head> of your HTML and looks like this: <meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow">.

The generator is a small form tool that builds this tag for you. You pick a robot name (usually robots for all crawlers, or googlebot if you only want to instruct Google), toggle the directives — index or noindex, follow or nofollow, plus advanced ones like nosnippet or max-image-preview — and the tool assembles the correct syntax instantly. For non-HTML files like PDFs, the same directives can be delivered through an X-Robots-Tag HTTP header set at the server, and some generators produce that version too.

It is worth understanding what this tag is not. It is different from robots.txt: robots.txt controls which paths crawlers may fetch at all, across the whole site, while the meta robots tag gives precise per-page instructions. A common beginner mistake is blocking a page in robots.txt and expecting noindex to work — if the crawler cannot fetch the page, it never sees the noindex directive. Per-page control lives in the tag, not the text file.

How to Use the Meta Robots Generator

First, choose who the tag speaks to: all robots, or a specific crawler like Googlebot. Then toggle your directives. The two everyone reaches for are index/noindex (should this page appear in search results?) and follow/nofollow (should crawlers follow the links on it and pass signals through them?). Meta Robots Directives Explained

Each directive is a single instruction. The ones I see used most, with plain-language meanings:

DirectiveMeaningTypical use
indexAllow the page in search resultsDefault for pages you want found
noindexKeep the page out of search resultsThank-you pages, admin areas, staging sites
followCrawlers may follow links and pass signalsDefault for normal content pages
nofollowDo not pass link signals through the page's linksPages with untrusted or paid links
noarchiveNo cached-copy link in resultsContent you do not want archived
nosnippetNo text snippet shown in resultsPrivate or sensitive listings
max-snippet:[n]Limit snippet to n charactersTight control over result previews
max-image-previewnone, standard, or large image previewControl image display in results

A few of these combine, and the more restrictive rule wins when they conflict. noindex, follow is a classic pairing: the page itself stays out of results, but crawlers still follow its links and pass signals onward — useful for category pages you want crawled but not listed. Google applies the stricter directive when tags disagree, so when in doubt, the restrictive option is the one that takes effect.

Real Examples of Generated Tags

Here is what the generator produces for the most common jobs:

Keep a login page out of search: <meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow"> — neither the page nor its links should be touched by search engines.

Deindex a thank-you page but keep link equity flowing: <meta name="robots" content="noindex, follow"> — the page stays hidden from results while crawlers still follow its outgoing links.

Speak only to Google about snippets: <meta name="googlebot" content="nosnippet"> — targets one crawler and leaves the default index/follow in place.

Cap snippet length for news content: <meta name="robots" content="index, follow, max-snippet:-1, max-image-preview:large"> — full snippets allowed with large image previews, common for articles chasing rich results.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a meta robots tag?

An HTML tag in the page's head that tells search engines whether to index the page, follow its links, show snippets, or display image and video previews — instructions apply per page.

When should I use noindex?

Use noindex for pages users should reach but search engines should not list: thank-you pages, login and admin areas, staging sites, internal search results, and thin or duplicate content. The page must stay crawlable — never also block it in robots.txt, or the crawler will never see the noindex.

What is the difference between robots.txt and the meta robots tag?

robots.txt controls crawling across whole paths (what crawlers may fetch); the meta robots tag controls indexing of a single page (what search engines may show). For noindex to work, the page must be crawlable so the crawler can read the tag.

Can nofollow in the meta robots tag affect my rankings?

nofollow tells crawlers not to pass link signals through the page's links. It does not harm the page itself; it simply stops the page from distributing equity to its outbound links.

How do I verify the tag is working?

View the page source to confirm the tag is in the head, then use Search Console's URL inspection tool or an indexability checker to confirm how Google reads the directive.

Is this generator free?

Yes — completely free, no account needed, unlimited tags.

Does the tool change my website?

No. It only generates the tag text in your browser. You paste it into your page yourself.

Should I use noindex or delete the page?

If the page serves users (a login screen, a thank-you page), keep it and noindex it. If it serves nobody and has no links pointing to it, deletion plus a redirect is usually cleaner.
