What Is a Text to ASCII Converter?

ASCII is the standard chart mapping 128 common characters to numbers 0–127. Every letter you type is one of those numbers under the hood: capital letters live at 65–90, lowercase at 97–122, digits at 48–57, and punctuation is scattered through the rest. This tool reads your text character by character and prints the number behind each one.

It is the reverse of decoding. Programmers use these codes when building encoders, when debugging string handling, or when a protocol demands numeric character values instead of text. Students meet it in the same computer science classes that teach binary — the ASCII chart is the bridge between letters and numbers, and this tool is the fast lane across it.

What I appreciate is the flexibility of the output. Decimal codes (72 101 108 108 111) are the default, but good tools also offer hex (48 65 6C 6C 6F) and binary (01001000 01100101) views of the same text — three encodings of one message, side by side.

How to Use the Text to ASCII Converter

Type or paste your text into the input box. Click Convert and each character appears as its decimal ASCII code, usually separated by spaces. Copy the list to your clipboard or download it as a text file for your code, homework, or puzzle.

If you are learning, try encoding your own name first — watching familiar letters turn into numbers makes the chart feel concrete. Developers debugging byte-level issues often encode just the suspicious substring to compare against the bytes their program actually produced; a mismatch between expected and actual codes pinpoints the bug fast.

One habit worth building: watch for invisible characters. A trailing space (32) or newline (10) at the end of your input will show up as an extra code, and spotting it here explains a surprising number of "why doesn't this string match" mysteries.

How Text to ASCII Conversion Works

One lookup per character — the simplest pipeline in the converter family.

Step 1 — Read each character. The tool walks through your text one character at a time, including spaces, punctuation, and line breaks.

Step 2 — Chart lookup. Each character is replaced by its ASCII decimal value: H → 72, e → 101, l → 108, o → 111, space → 32.

Step 3 — Join with separators. The codes are joined with spaces (or commas, or newlines — your choice) so each number stays distinct.

Worked example — encode "Hi!": H → 72, i → 105, ! → 33 → output "72 105 33". Encoding a whole paragraph takes milliseconds; the tool also handles the reverse check — decode your output with an ASCII-to-text converter and your original text should come back exactly.

Frequently asked

Is this text to ASCII converter free?

Yes — free, no account, no limits.

What is the ASCII code for A?

65. Lowercase a is 97, the digit 0 is 48, and a space is 32.

What is the difference between text to ASCII and text to binary?

Text to ASCII gives decimal codes (72 101); text to binary gives the 8-bit binary form (01001000 01100101). Same characters, different number bases.

Why does "1" encode as 49, not 1?

The tool encodes the character "1" (ASCII 49), not the number 1. Text digits and numeric values are different things.

Can I encode emoji or non-English text?

Standard ASCII covers codes 0–127 (extendable to 255). Emoji and other scripts need UTF-8 encoding, which produces multi-byte sequences.

Is my data secure?

Yes. Encoding happens entirely in your browser — your text never leaves your device.

How do I convert the codes back to text?

Use the matching ASCII-to-text tool: paste the decimal codes and decode each through the chart.

What is the ASCII code for a newline?

10 (line feed). Windows line endings use the pair 13, 10.
